01

Public records

Customer visibility + queue accountability
Recommended first

“Where is my police report?”

A request has sat in Initial Review for 13 days.

Who is waiting
A resident who received a confirmation but no useful explanation.
What is stuck
The request is far beyond its two-business-day target. No accountable owner or blocker has been recorded.
What HossTracker shows
The overdue stage, missing owner, event history, next action, largest queue, and a customer-safe status.
What remains authorized
A supervisor must review the evidence before assigning an owner, escalating the item, or publishing an update.

02

Military dental readiness

Unit ↔ civilian dental office
Cross-organization handoff

“Hoss this dental verification.”

A unit needs a civilian dentist to return one completed form.

Who is waiting
A Soldier and readiness team need confirmation; the dental office needs a clear request and return path.
What is stuck
A DD 2813 request is moving through email, attachments, phone calls, and “did you receive it?” follow-ups.
What HossTracker shows
One structured handoff with the request, deadline, receipt, questions, document return, and shared status—without joining databases.
What remains authorized
Authorized unit and dental-office personnel decide whether to send, accept, upload, disclose, or complete anything.

03

Healthcare prior authorization

Patient + provider + insurer
Appropriate views for each role

“Why is this authorization stuck?”

The insurer is waiting for clinical documentation the provider believes it sent.

Who is waiting
A patient waiting for care, a provider waiting for a decision, and an insurer waiting for complete evidence.
What is stuck
The missing-document request has not reached the right person, while every party sees a different partial status.
What HossTracker shows
The dependency, owner, deadline, question and response—plus separate patient, provider, and insurer views with only appropriate detail.
What remains authorized
Qualified personnel decide what information may be requested, shared, accepted, or used to make the authorization decision.
