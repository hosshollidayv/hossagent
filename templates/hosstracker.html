Operational truthSix questions
  1. 01
    Where is it?

    Current workflow stage and time in that stage.

  2. 02
    Who owns it?

    An accountable person or team—not an ambiguous queue.

  3. 03
    What happens next?

    The next action required to move the work forward.

  4. 04
    When should that happen?

    A contractual target, operational target, or clearly bounded estimate.

  5. 05
    What is blocking it?

    A recorded dependency, with absence described as absence of evidence.

  6. 06
    When should completion be expected?

    A responsible range when history supports one—never invented precision.

Operational truth above systems of record

Make invisible queues legible.

HossTracker is designed to sit above or alongside existing systems, preserving their authority while making ownership, timing, exceptions, and next actions useful to the people waiting on the work.

01

Medicine Cabinet

Separate healthy, at-risk, overdue, blocked, and unowned work so a manager can understand operational health at a glance.

02

Business-time SLA

Measure stage age against calendar or business-time targets, with configured holidays and pauses—without silently stopping a clock.

03

Responsible ETA

Use historical stage duration to show a range and confidence when the evidence supports it; say so plainly when it does not.

04

Role-safe status

Project the same work into separate internal, shared, and customer views so transparency does not become oversharing.

The Hoss Network

Email moves messages. HossTracker moves work.

Interoperability—not a giant shared database—is the proposed network effect. Each institution keeps its own item and its own internal truth. A narrowly scoped HossHandoff carries the context and agreed state between them.

Native path

One click when both sides use HossTracker.

The recipient receives a new internal item linked to the same global correlation ID. Each side can publish shared status without exposing its database or private workflow.

Graceful-degradation path

A counterparty should not need a deployment.

A secure link can open a lightweight external portal where the recipient acknowledges, responds, uploads a document, provides an ETA, asks a question, or completes the request. If that organization adopts later, the correlation history remains intact.

Network governance by design

Shared work. Separate authority.

A handoff grants access to one bounded shared surface. It does not grant one institution access to another institution's item, users, database, or internal decision process.

01

Scoped visibility

03

Attributable events
