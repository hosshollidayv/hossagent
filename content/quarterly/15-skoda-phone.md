Title: I Have a Škoda. I Have a Phone.
Date: 2026-10-04
Category: Arrival dispatches
Deck: Original Log 20: mobility and communications, finally in the same sentence.

The promised Golf turned out to be a Škoda. I regarded this as an acceptable development. The car had keys, room for things, and no apparent interest in becoming a lifestyle statement.

Keys are powerful after several days of temporary arrangements. A timetable asks you to coordinate with the future. A car key lets you leave now.

Then came the phone. At the exchange, a helpful employee took the accumulated confusion surrounding my mobile service and converted it into a functioning connection. Data worked. Calls worked. The number was mine.

Until that moment, the phone had been a highly capable glass object whose usefulness depended on finding someone else's Wi-Fi. Every errand came with an asterisk: could the landlord call, could the driver reach me, would the map remain available after I left the building?

Now the asterisks were gone.

From the provider's perspective, a plan had been activated. From mine, I could leave the hotel without arranging a digital lifeline in advance. That is the difference between describing a transaction and understanding its value.

A car and a phone sound like ordinary possessions because they are. Temporarily lose reliable access to both, and their return feels like the restoration of a small state.

I had a Škoda. I had a phone. The Signal Corps could have recognized the outline of a satisfactory afternoon.
