Title: Every Officer, a Product Manager?
Date: 2026-10-08
Category: Founder’s notes
Deck: An appealing comparison becomes useful only when it gets specific.

“Every Army officer is a product manager” is the sort of sentence that can become a LinkedIn post before anyone has checked what it means.

I understand the attraction. Both jobs involve priorities, constraints, people with competing needs, and responsibility that exceeds direct control. Both can also be performed badly through the energetic production of documents.

The useful comparison begins with the outcome. What needs to change for the people doing the work? What prevents that change now? Which assumptions are we making? Who has the expertise to challenge them?

A liaison officer encounters these questions at organizational boundaries. A product team encounters them wherever the customer's experience crosses a boundary the org chart has treated as the end of somebody's job.

The temptation in both settings is to receive a request and immediately organize its execution. Sometimes that is exactly right. Sometimes the request is one person's proposed solution to a problem the team has not yet understood. Speed in the wrong direction remains speed, which is why it photographs so well.

The work I value gives capable people a clear problem, useful context, and room to exercise judgment. It also asks for evidence that the result helped. A completed deliverable matters because of what it makes possible.

The comparison has limits. Military authority, obligations, and consequences are their own things. But the habit of understanding before prescribing travels well.

I would rather bring that habit into the room than introduce myself with a metaphor. The room probably has enough metaphors. It may need someone to ask a better question.
