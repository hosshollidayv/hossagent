Title: It Ain’t Luck
Date: 2026-10-02
Category: Arrival dispatches
Deck: Original Log 14: the planned pickup fails to appear; a useful person does.

I wanted a man holding a sign with my name on it.

This was not because Frankfurt lacks transportation. It was because, after enough travel, a sign reading HERR HOLLIDAY means someone else has solved the next twenty minutes. You may stop making decisions. You have been expected.

The expected pickup did not materialize within the time I gave it. I found another driver, loaded the expedition, and went to Wiesbaden.

He also provided a hotspot. At that moment, transportation and functioning internet were a remarkably complete description of civilization. No elaborate recovery plan was required. A competent person had a car, understood the destination, and was willing to help.

As the airport receded, it lost its institutional power. A few minutes earlier it had controlled every movement. Now it was a building behind us.

The original log called this “It Ain't Luck.” I still like the title, with one qualification: other people's competence and kindness deserve credit. Adaptability did not produce the car by force of character. I looked for another way forward, and someone made that way possible.

The practical lesson is smaller than a doctrine and more useful: give the intended arrangement a reasonable chance, then stop treating its absence as a prohibition on movement.

The guitars were aboard. The phone was connected. Wiesbaden was getting closer. That was enough strategy for one car ride.
