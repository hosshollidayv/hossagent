Title: Three Minutes Late
Date: 2026-10-02
Category: Arrival dispatches
Deck: Original Log 16: one man, one timetable, and insufficient corroborating passengers.

The shuttle was three minutes late. More concerningly, I was the only person waiting for it.

A crowd at a bus stop is a form of evidence. Twenty people staring down the road reassure you that your interpretation of the timetable has survived independent review. Alone, you are a transportation hypothesis with a backpack.

I checked the schedule again. The printed time remained unmoved by my attention. I considered the day, the direction, the stop, the possibility of a newer schedule, and several increasingly elaborate explanations for the absence of a bus.

Three minutes would not ordinarily have mattered. But expectations have geography. I had arrived in Germany prepared to believe in the authority of a printed number, and the printed number was currently receiving no support from traffic.

I was also tired. Fatigue can transform a minor uncertainty into a review of the institutions that produced it. The sensible question was whether to wait a little longer. My brain preferred an inquiry into the reliability of European civilization.

There were alternatives. I was in a city with cars, taxis, and sidewalks. This was useful to remember before the timetable became a religious text.

A bus stop is a small public commitment to a prediction. I stood beside mine and tried to look as though the evidence remained favorable.
