Title: Available, Except for the Electricity
Date: 2026-10-12
Category: Systems & people
Deck: An electric car meets a charging experience with strong opinions about the word available.

The charger existed. The car existed. Electricity was, by all available evidence, a mature technology.

The remaining task was to make these facts cooperate.

I encountered apps, payment attempts, account questions, and the sort of reassuring availability indicator that becomes less reassuring each time the session fails to start. The system could represent the opportunity to charge with considerable confidence. Delivering the charge was proving more difficult.

The experience had layers. A driver sees one transaction. Behind it sit equipment, connectivity, payment, authorization, and several organizations whose boundaries are invisible until something goes wrong. At that point the boundaries become the principal thing the customer learns.

Eventually I got the Volvo charging. The relief was ridiculous and entirely real. I had not discovered a new energy source. I had achieved the advertised behavior of a parking-space appliance.

I wanted the battery full. After so much negotiation with the charging process, stopping early felt emotionally premature.

There is a useful product question here: when a system says “available,” available to whom, under what conditions, and with what remaining steps? A technical state becomes a customer promise the moment it appears on a screen beside a button.

The car did not care about the explanation. It cared about receiving electricity. I had begun to share its view.

When the session finally worked, the experience became beautifully uneventful. That was the experience I had been trying to purchase all along.
