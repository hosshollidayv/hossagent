Title: The Glamour of Following Up
Date: 2026-10-26
Category: The liaison desk
Deck: Monday, a shorter brief, and the quiet satisfaction of closing the loop.

Monday's work consisted largely of doing the things that last week's work had made necessary.

This is an unpromising opening for a leadership memoir. No dramatic pivot. No revelation achieved while staring through rain at a European skyline. There were materials to review, coordination to finish, and an update to prepare. The revelation, if there was one, was that these activities improve substantially when somebody actually does them.

I finalized a staff update and the notes behind it. A concise brief is a peculiar object. The audience sees a small amount of information and may reasonably conclude that it required a small amount of work. Much of the work, of course, went into deciding what could be left out without making what remained misleading.

The temptation is to include every action. You made the calls. You found the material. You followed up. Surely the slide should acknowledge your existence. Unfortunately, the slide has a reader, and the reader has a decision to make. My desire for documentary recognition is not automatically relevant to that decision.

So the question becomes: what changed, why does it matter, and what needs attention next?

I also worked on the less visible infrastructure of coordination: knowing where to go for an answer, which contacts were established, and which gaps still needed validation. A list of names can look reassuringly complete long before it functions as a network. The important part is whether the next question reaches someone who can answer it.

This is where liaison work rewards a certain tolerance for the uncinematic. You retrieve the authoritative version. You review it. You pass the relevant information through the right channel. You identify what remains open. Then you do the thing that feels least like an accomplishment and is frequently the most useful: you follow up.

As a founder, I see the attraction of systems that promise to eliminate this work. As a practitioner, I am more interested in systems that make its state legible. Which action is still waiting? What is it waiting on? Who can move it? What changed since we last looked? Those questions can justify a tool. The mere existence of a busy person cannot.

The next day's task was to review another product and extract the actionable requirements. The word “actionable” is doing real work there. An observation does not become a task merely because it has entered my field of vision. Some things need awareness. Some need a decision. Some need a named person to do something by a particular time.

I would like my reporting to preserve those differences.

Monday ended with follow-on work completed and the next questions clearer. This may never be adapted for cinema. It is, however, a reasonable basis for coming back on Tuesday.
