Title: The Men Who Own the Dirt
Date: 2026-10-19
Category: The liaison desk
Deck: Training becomes real in the places where plans meet physical requirements.

A training organization is easy to describe at an altitude where everything becomes a capability. Descend far enough and there is ground, equipment, scheduling, maintenance, and someone who knows exactly why the attractive idea will require three other conversations first.

My early effort to understand the training enterprise kept bringing me back to those people.

A map can show a location. A slide can name a function. Neither tells you what must be coordinated for people to arrive and do useful work there. That knowledge lives partly in documents and partly in the heads of people who have seen similar plans meet similar constraints before.

These are the people I want to hear from early. They are sometimes mistaken for the people who say no. Often they are explaining the conditions under which yes could become real.

The distinction matters. An enthusiastic newcomer can hear a dependency as resistance. A more useful newcomer writes it down and asks who else needs to be involved.

Liaison work occupies that territory. The contribution is often to connect a requirement with the person who understands its practical consequences, then carry the answer back without sanding off the inconvenient parts.

The dirt is a useful corrective to abstraction. It reminds you that a capability is something people must actually be able to use, somewhere, under particular conditions. The map is only the beginning of the explanation.

I am still learning which questions to ask. Finding the people who know the ground seems like a good start.
