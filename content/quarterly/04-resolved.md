Title: Resolved, in the Administrative Sense
Date: 2026-10-22
Category: Systems & people
Deck: A meditation on the distance between a closed record and a completed outcome.

The word “resolved” has a pleasant finality. It suggests that something has happened and that you may now think about lunch.

In practice, it can describe the state of a record while leaving the state of the world available for further discussion. A step is complete. A referral is closed. A team has performed its assigned action. All of these things may be true. The person at the end of the process may still be waiting.

My notes today kept returning to that gap: the difference between evidence that a process has advanced and evidence that the required result has occurred. It is a small distinction until you are the person whose next task depends on it. Then it occupies most of the room.

The same pattern appears in the humble act of sharing a document. A link has been sent. This is a fact. Whether the intended recipient can open it is a different fact. The sender's outbox is a poor place to look for evidence of the recipient's success.

There is no villain required. One person follows the procedure, another works the queue, and a third receives a status that is accurate within a boundary the third person cannot see. The misunderstanding is assembled cooperatively by people doing their jobs.

Several conversations also reinforced a second lesson: the time to understand a dependency is before it becomes urgent. A requirement does not become simpler because we discover it late. Missing information remains missing, even after the email acquires a red exclamation mark and the subject line begins to resemble a warning label on industrial machinery.

The useful intervention is early and specific. What information is needed? Who supplies it? Who can act on it? What demonstrates that the action worked? A checklist earns its place when it helps answer those questions. It loses its place when completing the checklist becomes the thing we celebrate.

This is the territory that interests me as a builder. A status should tell the person relying on it something they can use. “Submitted” should mean submitted. “Awaiting another team” should name the dependency where appropriate. “Complete” should have a definition that survives contact with the person who needed the result.

Software cannot settle that definition on behalf of the organization. It can make the disagreement visible. That alone can be valuable, provided we resist the urge to paint the disagreement green.

My own discipline is to ask for the observable result and then check it. The question is not an accusation. It is an attempt to finish the work together.

Lunch remains an excellent ambition. I would simply like the word “resolved” to earn it.
