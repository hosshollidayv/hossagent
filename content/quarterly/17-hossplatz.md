Title: The HossPlatz
Date: 2026-10-04
Category: Systems & people
Deck: Original Log 22: you have a car. We have ground. Five euros.

I discovered a place to park for five euros a day and experienced an emotion normally reserved for more significant developments.

By then, nearby garage prices had begun to alter my understanding of what storing a car should cost. A sufficiently expensive environment can make a bad deal feel like a natural law. Then someone mentions a gravel lot and the law collapses.

Finding it required first entering the wrong garage. The ticket advertised charging privileges for Porsche drivers, which helped clarify that my rented Škoda and I had selected the wrong economic habitat.

Eventually I reached Paulinenhang. Trees, gravel, cars. At the time of my visit, five euros bought the day. I named it the HossPlatz, exercising a municipal authority that nobody had granted me.

Its proposition was unusually concise: you have a car; we have ground.

No part of the experience suggested that the parking lot aspired to become a platform. It was content to let my vehicle remain somewhere while I did other things. This was precisely what I had wanted, although several more expensive facilities had encouraged me to consider whether I also wanted elevators and architectural ambition.

The delight came from the removal of a concern. I could stop thinking about the car.

Earlier, after Mass, I had been studying the city's graffiti with the unsolicited interest of a product manager encountering a visible problem. The parking lot supplied a useful counterweight to that impulse. Sometimes a good solution is already there, doing its job quietly.

May it continue to do so. May nobody improve it into something I need an account to understand.
