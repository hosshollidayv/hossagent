Title: The Watch My Father Kept
Date: 2026-10-26
Category: After hours
Deck: A high-school purchase returns with more meaning than it had when it was new.

I bought the watch in high school, probably in the spring of 2007, with the sophisticated strategic objective of impressing a girl.

I think her father had the same one. His name was Andy, and he was a farrier. I remember the family as well off, which has left me with an enduring and entirely unresolved question about the economics of horseshoes. Perhaps Andy had built an excellent business. Perhaps he had inherited money and was shoeing horses for the love of the game. Cheers to Andy either way.

At seventeen, I did not need to solve this. I needed the watch.

Years passed. The clasp broke. The watch stopped doing what it was supposed to do. I thought it was lost. My father had kept it.

He nursed it back to working order and sent it to me for my birthday.

This is an inconvenient development for anyone attempting to reason about watches primarily through specifications. The old watch has acquired an advantage that cannot be added to a comparison table. My father kept something I had stopped keeping track of. Then he put effort into making it useful to me again.

It is now my work watch.

The younger version of me wanted it to suggest a certain kind of adulthood. The current version wears it with a different understanding of the adults who were already there: a father who held onto it, and another man whose watch once seemed to contain an explanation of how the world worked.

I still enjoy looking at watches. Beautiful little machines are allowed to be beautiful little machines. But this one returned with a history I did not know it was accumulating.

The seventeen-year-old bought it hoping somebody would notice. At thirty-six, I wear it because somebody remembered.
