Title: Six for Six
Date: 2026-10-19
Category: After hours
Deck: Six venue inquiries. A booking rate yet to be established.

I sent six inquiries to local music venues.

This produced the satisfying phrase “six for six,” which requires immediate qualification. Six messages had been sent. Six gigs had not been booked. The distinction is particularly important when a product manager is doing his own publicity.

The offer was straightforward: a Wiesbaden-based American singer and guitarist, playing country, blues, and improvised jazz. I had instruments, material, and an unreasonable willingness to discuss amplification. The venues had calendars and audiences. The next step was to discover whether any of those things belonged together.

One response said the act was not a fit for the venue's regular concerts and pointed me toward a guitar club. That was useful. A thoughtful referral can do more than an encouraging message that leaves you exactly where you started.

I emphasized that I was local. This mattered because it was true and because it changed the practical proposition. I was not asking someone to accommodate an international tour. I was in town, ready to play, and interested in becoming part of its musical life.

The Army assignment had provided a place to work. Music offered another way to belong to the city.

There is no need to turn six emails into a heroic outreach doctrine. I made a clear offer, supplied something people could hear, and accepted that the answer might be no or might lead somewhere I had not considered.

Six for six, then, in the narrow and honorable sense that I had asked. The rest belonged to the people receiving the messages.
