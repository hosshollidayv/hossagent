Title: The Guest List
Date: 2026-10-26
Category: After hours
Deck: The Christmas Miracle Edition. A sold-out show, a modest request, and seven very good words.

“You’re on the guestlist, merry Christmas.”

Alex Fang sent seven words. I have received considerably longer messages that accomplished less.

The Revival Tour show in Wiesbaden was sold out. I had discovered it late, which is one of the more reliable ways to become deeply interested in an event. The music mattered to me. The venue was in the city where I was now living. The ticket situation had other plans.

I asked around. The request was simple: did anyone know of a spare ticket or a late-release allocation? I was happy to pay face value. I wanted to get into the room and hear the music.

One of those messages went to Dave Hause's team. They were not on the tour, but they replied that they could ask the promoter. On October 23, they forwarded the request. On October 26, Alex's reply arrived.

That is the complete mechanism, and it is better than a complicated one. Somebody took a small amount of trouble on behalf of someone they did not need to help.

I am tempted, professionally, to explain this as liaison work. A request crosses a boundary, reaches the right person, and produces an outcome. The temptation should be handled carefully. A kindness does not become more valuable because I can put it in the vocabulary of my job.

What matters is that the ask was human, the response was generous, and nobody treated the absence of an obligation as a reason to stop being helpful.

The concert is still ahead of me. As I write this, I have not walked through the door or heard the first song. The miracle is currently an email and the prospect of a December evening that had appeared unavailable.

I sent my thanks. Dave's team deserves them for passing the request along. Alex deserves them for making it happen.

Christmas arrived early, in a sentence short enough to fit comfortably on a phone screen. For a man spending much of October discovering how many messages a simple outcome can require, it was a particularly elegant gift.
