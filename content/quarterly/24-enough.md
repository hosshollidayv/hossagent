Title: Have I Done Enough?
Date: 2026-10-22
Category: Founder’s notes
Deck: The end-of-week question that a crowded calendar cannot answer.

“Have I done enough?” is a reasonable question at the end of a week. It becomes less reasonable when I attempt to answer it by counting every email, meeting, and small administrative victory available to the defense.

A liaison officer can be busy in ways that are difficult to explain and useful in ways that do not produce much visible activity. A brief conversation may settle a confusion. A long thread may preserve it in a more durable format.

The week had produced relationships, clearer distinctions, questions answered, and follow-up work identified. The useful report should say what changed and what needed attention. It should not require the reader to inspect the entire machinery of my establishment as a person in a building.

I caught myself wanting the report to certify effort. This is understandable. A new assignment comes with uncertainty, and a detailed account of one's activity can function as a small insurance policy against the fear of appearing idle.

But the people receiving it need useful information. They do not need to carry my uncertainty as an attachment.

So I preferred the concise version. The work was there. The next actions were there. The personal setup details could remain where they belonged.

The same discipline matters in product work. A team deserves recognition for effort, but effort alone cannot tell us whether customers are better off. We need to understand what changed for them and what remains unresolved.

There is a humane version of this standard. It does not demand a miracle every Friday. It asks for an honest account of progress, including uncertainty, without padding it into an achievement ceremony.

Enough for the report. Tomorrow's work can have tomorrow.
