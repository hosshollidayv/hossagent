Title: The Remarkable Persistence of Captain Benjamin Driss
Date: 2026-10-21
Category: The liaison desk
Deck: A name in the coordination chain turns out to have been in the story for twenty years.

I recognized the name.

Captain Benjamin Driss was not simply another person in the growing list of people who might help me understand the work. He was Ben Driss. Blue Ridge High School Ben Driss. The Ben from the Monte Cristo lunch-duel story.

There is a particular kind of disbelief that occurs when a person from your teenage life reappears inside a professional system that normally feels impersonal. For a moment, the title and the name refuse to occupy the same sentence. Then they settle together, and you realize that time has been quietly promoting everyone.

The old lunch story belongs to the archive. I will spare Ben a reconstructed version assembled from uncertain details. The important fact is that I had known him before either of us had become the kind of person whose assistance might be requested in a staff coordination chain.

Now he was helping me find information and understand where to look. Useful work, carried out by a person who arrived with an unexpected second biography.

An organization is never as anonymous as its charts suggest. Every box contains someone who went to school somewhere, ate lunch with somebody, and has a version of himself that does not appear beside his duty title.

This is easy to forget when you mostly encounter people as names in email. Recognition restores the missing dimensions all at once.

I came to Germany expecting to establish new working relationships. Apparently the personnel system had also arranged a reunion.

Of all the things that could have survived the journey from Blue Ridge to a headquarters in Germany, I had not expected the lunch-duel cast list. Yet here we were, older, useful to one another, and somehow still in the same story.
