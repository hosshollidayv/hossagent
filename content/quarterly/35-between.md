Title: The Man Between Two Headquarters
Date: 2026-10-22
Category: The liaison desk
Deck: A liaison officer considers the territory between the boxes.

A liaison officer's office can be located on a floor plan. The work is harder to place.

Much of it occurs between organizations: where a requirement has been identified but not understood by everyone it affects, where information exists but has not reached the person who needs it, or where two reasonable assumptions are quietly preparing to collide.

I spent the week learning those spaces.

The job carries an obvious temptation to become a human forwarding rule. Receive information. Send information. Accumulate evidence of circulation. This can produce a busy inbox while leaving the important uncertainty intact.

The more useful question is what the recipient can do with what arrives. Is it current? Is the context sufficient? Is there an actual request? Does someone own the next step? If the message produces another round of basic clarification, perhaps I had more work to do before pressing send.

Relationships help because they make clarification possible before the misunderstanding hardens. A short call can reveal that two people mean different things by the same word. It can also reveal that neither needs another meeting; they need a decision from a third person.

My contribution is not to occupy every conversation. It is to understand enough of the work to recognize which conversations need to happen.

The best version of this role may be almost invisible afterward. People have what they need. A dependency is understood early. A question reaches the right desk. Nothing dramatic occurs because something useful occurred first.

That can be difficult to summarize at the end of a day. I am trying to become comfortable with the fact that the absence of a preventable problem does not come with a receipt.
