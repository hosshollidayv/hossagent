Title: Operation Little Tweed
Date: 2026-10-17
Category: After hours
Deck: A small amplifier enters a household already equipped to be heard.

I bought the little tweed Champ.

The word “little” is doing protective work in that sentence. It suggests modesty, restraint, and an object that can be explained without clearing the evening's schedule. The amplifier is physically small. My ability to think about amplifiers had shown no such limitation.

I already had the Jazz Chorus, an excellent piece of equipment with the dimensions and civic presence of a minor public building. The Champ offered a different proposition: a simple box, a speaker, and a direct relationship between the guitar and the room.

This was not a replacement. Guitarists rarely permit such a wasteful interpretation of a purchase. It was a different useful thing, a phrase whose capacity to accommodate additional equipment should concern anyone sharing our finances.

Later, the attenuator helped, but it did not abolish the relationship between volume and the sound I wanted. The amplifier still benefited from being allowed to breathe. A distortion pedal could supply a more immediate bark, although then I was enjoying a different sound again.

There is no need to force this into a theory of enterprise software. I like playing the guitar. I like how the two amplifiers respond differently. A year away from home needs some ordinary pleasures that remain pleasures rather than evidence of professional development.

The Champ worked. I played it. For once, that was the complete acceptance test.
