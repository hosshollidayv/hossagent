Title: The Garrison of No Electrons
Date: 2026-10-15
Category: Systems & people
Deck: Adapted from Log 34: the car is electric; the plan must account for that.

My car required electricity. I had selected an electric car, so this should not have surprised me. What required adjustment was the assumption that charging would fit conveniently around the rest of the day.

The guidance I received was that I needed to use public charging rather than treat the installation as part of my personal energy plan. This was a constraint. A constraint does not become a service failure merely because I had not incorporated it into my preferred arrangement.

I incorporated it, with commentary.

Meanwhile, I was investigating amplifiers, devices whose relationship with electricity is generally clearer. You supply power. They make sound. My research concerned which sound I wanted and how much of it an apartment could reasonably accommodate. The phrase “small amplifier” is open to interpretation by everyone except the neighbors.

The two inquiries occupied the same month: how to put enough energy into the car, and how to keep the guitar from putting too much energy into the building.

A person can become extremely modern in one purchase and deliberately old-fashioned in the next. Electric transport. Simple guitar amplifier. Apps for one, knobs for the other. I do not regard this as an inconsistency. I regard it as having to use the things.

The useful question is what the complete experience demands. A convenient vehicle arrangement still needs a charging routine. A beautiful amplifier still needs to work in the room where it will be played.

The missing feature is sometimes a plan. Annoying, but cheaper to discover before purchasing another accessory.
